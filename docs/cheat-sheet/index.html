How cells are made

  1. Electrode manufacturing — mixing → coating → drying → calendering → slitting → vacuum drying
  2. Cell assembly, in a dry room at a dew point of about −30 to −60 °C — separation → stacking or winding → tab welding → packaging → electrolyte filling → wetting
  3. Cell finishing — formation (first charge at about 0.1–0.5C; builds the SEI) → degassing → aging (up to about three weeks; screens for internal shorts) → end-of-line testing and grading

Calendering sets porosity. A burr from slitting can short a cell. Cells shipped alone by air: at most 30 % state of charge.

Why cells age

MechanismFeeds mainlyAccelerated by
Corrosionimpedance ↑temperature
SEI growthcapacity ↓temperature, time, high state of charge
Gascapacity ↓, safetytemperature, overcharge
Surface depositsimpedance ↑ (power fade)cycling
Lithium platingcapacity ↓; self-discharge ↑ and shorts if dendrites growcold charging, high current
Crackingcapacity ↓ and impedance ↑deep cycling

Three signals: capacity fade, impedance rise, self-discharge rise. Heat speeds most aging; cold charging adds plating — so the best temperature window is moderate.
