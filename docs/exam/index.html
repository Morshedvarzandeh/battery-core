1 What does the separator let through?

The separator is an electronic insulator whose pores are filled with electrolyte, so ions pass and electrons are forced round the external circuit. Review in Lesson 1 →

2 While a cell is being charged, which electrode is the anode?

The anode is where oxidation happens. During charging the charger pulls electrons out of the positive electrode, so it is the anode. Industry still calls graphite “the anode”, after its role on discharge. Review in Lesson 1 →

3 Why is the negative current collector made of copper rather than aluminium?

Each metal survives only on its own side: aluminium alloys with lithium at low potential, and copper would dissolve at the positive electrode’s high potential. Review in Lesson 1 →

4 Pressing an electrode to a lower porosity mainly trades what for what?

More active material fits in each cubic centimetre, but the electrolyte-filled pores become narrower and more winding, so ions move more slowly. Review in Lesson 1B →

5 In a finished lithium-ion cell, where is the liquid electrolyte? Select every place.

Select every answer that applies.

The electrolyte fills the connected pores of both electrodes and the separator. The metal foils are dense and hold none. Review in Lesson 1B →

6 A 50 Ah cell is discharged at 2C. What is the current?

I = C-rate × capacity = 2 h⁻¹ × 50 Ah = 100 A. Review in Lesson 2 →

7 What is the ideal duration of a full discharge at C/5?

C/5 is a C-rate of 0.2 h⁻¹, and the ideal duration is 1 / 0.2 = 5 hours. Review in Lesson 2 →

8 A 20 Ah LFP cell has a nominal voltage of 3.2 V. What is its nominal energy?

Energy = capacity × nominal voltage = 20 Ah × 3.2 V = 64 Wh. Review in Lesson 2 →

9 Why does a real cell often run for less than the ideal duration at high current?

The arithmetic stays exact. A real discharge stops at a cutoff voltage, and the larger voltage losses at high current bring that cutoff forward. Review in Lesson 2 →

10 Which production step builds the solid electrolyte interphase (SEI)?

The SEI forms during the first controlled charges, which is why formation starts at a low current. Review in Lesson 3 →

11 Why is cell assembly done in a dry room?

Moisture is the enemy once the electrolyte is involved: it forms acid and gas inside the cell. Dust is handled by clean-room standards, not by drying the air. Review in Lesson 3 →

12 Which property of an electrode does calendering set most directly?

Calendering presses the dried coating to a defined thickness, which fixes its porosity. Coat weight is set earlier, at the coater. Review in Lesson 3 →

13 What is the main purpose of the aging step at the end of the line?

Aging is mainly a screen: a tiny internal short slowly discharges a cell, and only time makes that small voltage difference visible. Review in Lesson 3 →

14 Moving to an all-solid-state cell, which conventional step disappears?

The electrolyte is built into the stack as a solid layer, so there is nothing to inject and no pores to soak. Review in Lesson 3B →

15 Why does a sulfide solid-electrolyte line need a very dry or inert atmosphere?

Moisture degrades sulfide electrolytes and releases hydrogen sulfide, so the atmosphere protects both people and material. Review in Lesson 3B →

16 Near room temperature, how much faster does a degradation reaction with an activation energy of about 50 kJ/mol run when the cell is 10 °C warmer?

From the Arrhenius law, a reaction near 50 kJ/mol roughly doubles its rate for every +10 °C around room temperature. Review in Lesson 4 →

17 An SEI film is 12 nm thick after one year. Under the same conditions, how thick is it after four years?

The film grows with the square root of time, because it is its own diffusion barrier: four times the time gives twice the thickness. Review in Lesson 4 →

18 Which operating condition most favours lithium plating?

Cold slows the uptake of lithium into graphite while the charger keeps pushing current, so lithium is deposited as metal on the surface. Review in Lesson 4 →

19 Under the same load current, a cell’s voltage now sags more than when it was new. Which effect is that?

A larger voltage drop at the same current means more internal resistance. Capacity fade is fewer ampere-hours; self-discharge is charge lost while resting. Review in Lesson 4 →

20 By convention, at what remaining capacity is a cell said to have reached end of life?

80 % of rated capacity is the usual end-of-life line in datasheets and cycle-life tests; stationary batteries are often run further. Review in Lesson 4 →
